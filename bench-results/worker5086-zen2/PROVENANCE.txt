## What it decides, and it is not what was expected

**The pool wins and should ship. The guard should not.** Both looked good against
`base` on their own; the *joint* arm is what shows they conflict, and without it both
would have shipped and small threaded contractions would have lost ~3x. That is A20
stated positively -- the joint arm is the one that decides.

Drift-corrected treatment/base geomeans (each row deflated by its own `t1`, which no
switch can touch):

              pool t4   pool t64 | guard t4  guard t64 | both t4  both t64
  0.25 f64       1.53      10.30 |    0.61       7.86  |   0.63      8.48
  0.25 f32       1.77      11.64 |    0.73      10.84  |   0.79     12.50
    16 f64       0.99       2.38 |    0.99       1.08  |   1.01      2.41
    16 c32       1.02       2.27 |    1.00       1.02  |   1.01      2.40

and `both / pool` at 0.25 MiB reaches **0.32** (f64, t16): once threads are pooled
the guard is pure loss, because it rations a cost that no longer exists.

The pool's sub-0.90 tail is 0.7-1.4% of points, which is what A39's floor already

## Three findings that were not the point of the run

1. **The base arm replicates part 16 on a fourth Zen2 node.** 16 MiB `f64` reads
   best 6.20@t32 / t64 5.00 here against worker5139's 6.19@t32 / 5.03@t64. Two
   within-session ratios agreeing to 0.6%, which is what makes the treatment arm
   beside it trustworthy.
2. **The offline calibration of D48 replicates too**, simulated from this node's own
   base grid: no guard 0.20/564 slower, C=3e6 1.65/0 slower, oracle 2.37 -- against
   worker5139's 0.20/565, 1.66/0, 2.40. The *simulation* is sound; what it could not
   see is below.
3. **Part 16's "benign bandwidth ceiling above 1 MiB" is substantially wrong.** In
   all eight size x dtype rows, pooled `t64` beats unpooled *best-at-any-width*: at
   16 MiB `f64`, unpooled peaks at 6.20@t32 and pooled reaches 13.19@t32. Much of
   what was attributed to bandwidth was fixed cost.

## And the cost removed is more than thread spawn -- but the excess is unexplained

Median per-call time removed at t64, divided by 64:

  0.25 MiB  37.2 us/thread (f64c64)   37.2 (f32c32)
     1 MiB  43.4                      41.5
     4 MiB  54.0                      49.8
    16 MiB  93.6                      66.2

A spawn cost is size-independent. At 0.25 MiB this lands at the top of A43's
20-36 us -- an independent confirmation of it -- and by 16 MiB it is 2.5x that.

**Read that column with care: it is partly an artefact.** It divides the whole
pooled-vs-unpooled difference by the thread count, which assumes a pure fixed cost. The
difference is neither purely fixed (2.4 -> 6.0 ms) nor purely proportional (ratio
9.6 -> 2.1), so dividing manufactures some of the growth.

The per-thread packed-`A` buffer was offered as the mechanism and is **refuted from
this same data**: `ap_len` is identical at all four sizes (65536 reals in f64, 22528 in
c64, 135168 in f32, 46080 in c32), because `mc` never binds against `m` at these
shapes. Do not hoist those allocations expecting a win. Note also the trap that turned
up in checking it -- the `mc` in the `notes` column is the *derived* blocking from
`plan_config`, not the capped value the driver allocates from (`driver.rs:404`, `:445`)
-- so any footprint analysis from these CSVs must apply the cap itself.

What is solid: a fixed ~37 us/thread. The size-scaling component is unexplained;
scheduler placement of fresh threads and barrier skew are untested candidates.

Not comparable to anything measured on ccqlin038, and the absolute speedups are not
comparable to part 16's -- different node. Every ratio here is within-session.

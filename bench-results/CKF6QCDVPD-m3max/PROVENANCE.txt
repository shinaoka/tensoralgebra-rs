The portable path on Apple Silicon, and the first non-x86 measurement here.

  machine : CKF6QCDVPD (a laptop, not a cluster node), Apple M3 Max (T6031)
            aarch64-apple-darwin, macOS 25.5, 12 P-cores (2 clusters of 6)
            + 4 E-cores, no SMT
            P-core: 128 KiB L1d private, 16 MiB L2 shared by 6 cores,
            NO L3 that Darwin names (a ~48 MiB SLC exists, unexported),
            128-byte lines, 16 KiB pages, 4.05 GHz turbo on AC power
  date    : 2026-08-06
  script  : scripts/macos-session.sh prep|bench, size 64, reps 3
  engine  : kernel::scalar at MR x NR = 4 x 4 real / 2 x 4 complex.
            THERE IS NO NEON KERNEL. `TENSORCONTRACT_KERNEL` is a no-op here.
  full    : DECISIONS.md part 20

WHAT THIS DATA IS GOOD FOR

  The cost of the portable path, the three complex methods compared under an
  identical kernel, the corpus's structural features at MR = 4, and correctness
  on a new architecture. That is all.

WHAT IT CANNOT SUPPORT, AND THIS IS NOT BOILERPLATE

  * **These are not pinned single-core measurements.** Every other number in
    this repository is. Darwin has no CPU affinity API and
    `thread_policy_set(THREAD_AFFINITY_POLICY)` is a no-op on Apple Silicon, so
    there is no `taskset` equivalent and none can be written. Add a laptop's
    DVFS and thermal envelope, and 12 P-cores beside 4 E-cores the scheduler may
    migrate between. Compensations applied: AC power (the turbo needs it), a
    quiesced machine, thermal level recorded at both ends of the session,
    everything pinned single-threaded, and three identical arms instead of one.
    None of them is a substitute. The one thing better here than on a cluster
    node: no SMT, so the sibling-shares-L1d hazard does not exist.
  * **No number here may be compared with a number from another machine.**
    Different architecture as well as a different hierarchy.
  * **Nothing here is evidence about Apple Silicon as hardware.** It is evidence
    about the portable path on Apple Silicon. The two differ by about 2x (A58).

THE ACCELERATE FILE IS NOT A ROOFLINE. DO NOT MERGE IT WITH THE OPENBLAS ONE.

  premise-openblas-f64c64.csv     <- the roofline. OpenBLAS 0.3.34, NEON
  premise-accelerate-f64c64.csv   <- OFF-ISA. Apple AMX. A ceiling nothing
                                     NEON can reach, this engine included

  The measurement proves it rather than assuming it. Accelerate's TTGT reaches a
  geomean 79.2 GF/s in f64 and 99.4 in c64 and PEAKS AT 334.8, against a P-core
  NEON f64 FMA peak of 64.8 GF/s. 5.2x the peak is not NEON. OpenBLAS on the
  same shapes reads 30.7 and 35.0. So A3's "same-shape vendor GEMM as the
  achievable ceiling" is a different quantity against the two, and a ratio
  against Accelerate is not an efficiency number in the sense used everywhere
  else in this repository.

  A free control fell out of running premise twice: the engine columns cannot be
  touched by a BLAS swap, and they moved <=0.5% (f64 19.84 -> 19.74 GF/s,
  c64 24.20 -> 24.01). Rule 4, and the only floor figure this file had at the
  time the session was interrupted.

HEADLINE NUMBERS — AND EACH ONE COMES WITH ITS SIZE

  A46 exists because a one-shot small-size run produced a 1.68x that vanished at
  the published size. So: the numbers to quote are the premise ones, at 64 MiB.

  premise-openblas-f64c64.csv, 12 shapes, 64 MiB, reps 3, 1 thread, geomean GF/s:

                              f64                  c64
    engine (scalar)          19.84       planar 24.20 / 1m 23.32 / 3m 29.74
    OpenBLAS TTGT (NEON)     30.68                    35.02
    engine / OpenBLAS         0.65            0.69 planar, 0.85 3m

  In f64 all three methods read 19.84 EXACTLY, because they select the same real
  kernel. That is a control on the harness, not a coincidence.

  Best-shape efficiency, which is what identifies the mechanism:

    engine   26.07 of 32.4 GF/s  = 80.5%   <- ceiling HALVED by the missing FMA
    OpenBLAS 56.44 of 64.8 GF/s  = 87.1%   <- the full NEON FMA ceiling

  Two independently measured efficiencies agreeing under one frequency
  assumption (4.05 GHz P-core turbo, AC power). That agreement is the evidence,
  more than either number alone. Do NOT apply the 32.4 ceiling to the complex
  columns: c64 3m reads 36.94, i.e. 114% of it, because useful flops for 3m
  exceed the products it issues. That is 3m working, not an error.

  The gap is ONE INSTRUCTION FORM, not a missing kernel: LLVM vectorises
  real_ukr to NEON unasked, but will not contract `acc += a * b` into `fmla`
  because that is two roundings and IEEE forbids it. See A58.

  PROVISIONAL, AND LABELLED: a separate probe -- one case (ij-ik-kj), --size 8,
  reps 1 -- read engine 25.9, OpenBLAS-TTGT 54.5, TBLIS 2.0-dev 55.8 in f64,
  i.e. 0.46x of TBLIS. It is one case at one size and there is no TBLIS number
  at 64 MiB, because the TBLIS sweep did not run. Do not promote 0.46 to the
  headline until a 64 MiB sweep says so.

  Complex methods, premise geomean over 12 cases, c64, 64 MiB:

    3m      29.74 GF/s      <- leads planar by 1.229
    planar  24.20
    1m      23.32

  A THIRD ordering, after Cascade Lake's and Ice Lake's. It is a fact about the
  crippled kernel, not about the machine: with no FMA the kernel is
  instruction-throughput-bound, so 3m's 25% saving in products is a saving in
  the binding resource, and the bytes-per-useful-flop accounting that decides it
  on x86 is not what binds. EXPECT THIS TO MOVE once a NEON FMA kernel exists.
  See A59. In f64 all three methods read 19.84 exactly, because they select the
  same real kernel — a control on the harness, not a coincidence.

STRUCTURAL

  orient.csv: reg_a = 1.000 and reg_b = 1.000 on 784 of 784 rows. The corpus
  really is fully regular here, because MR = 4 divides the 24 that TCCG rounds
  every stride-1 extent up to. CLAUDE.md's 42.9%-below-1.0 is an x86
  register-block artefact needing MR in {16, 32, 48}. So `--stress ragged` is
  the ONLY source of irregularity on this machine, and any gather-path or
  write-back claim taken here without it is a claim about the regular case.

  `tcbench shapes` is absent on purpose: it emits a header and no rows off x86,
  because `row_blocks` returns `&[]`. `TENSORCONTRACT_ROWBLOCK` is inert too.

What this machine actually is, from the engine's own probes:

  Intel Ice Lake-SP, 2 x 32 cores, SMT off
  L1d 48 KiB 12-way private   (Cascade Lake: 32 KiB 8-way)
  L2  1280 KiB 20-way private (Cascade Lake: 1024 KiB 16-way)
  L3  48 MiB shared by 32 cores, i.e. one domain per socket
      (Cascade Lake: 25 MiB / 8 cores; Zen2: 16 MiB / 4 cores)
  avx2 fma avx512f avx512dq

The one-L3-per-socket topology is why this session ran STAGES="shapes threads" and
skipped the blocking grid: at most one or two arms can be placed concurrently, so
the 36-job grid would have been a ten-hour sequential run, and the placement
validation would have had nothing to validate.

Why it matters -- two results, and both are refutations:

  * **A34.** `kernel-shapes.txt` here disagrees with the same sweep on Cascade
    Lake by up to 13% on three of eight shipped register blocks, with the same
    ISA and the same 32 registers. Each machine prefers the other's loser by
    about 9%, and `real` wants NR+1 in both precisions -- a coherent signature,
    not scatter. Ice Lake's 48 KiB 12-way L1d accommodates an accumulator
    footprint Cascade Lake's 32 KiB 8-way does not. So register blocks are a
    property of the **microarchitecture**, not of the instruction set, and
    dispatch currently selects them by ISA alone. On every Ice Lake machine this
    engine runs a `real` kernel 9.2% off its own optimum today.
  * **A36.** The 4.3x that a 1-D N partition wins on Zen2's memory-bound family
    is simply absent here (pn/rule 1.014 at 32 threads), and the same code
    scales twice as well -- 10.6x in f64 and 15.3x in c64 3m at 32 cores,
    still climbing, against a 0.4-0.6% floor. One L3 serves the whole socket
    here, so the effect follows how many L3 domains the shared packed-B panel is
    spread across. The first machine alone would have produced the wrong rule.

Not comparable to anything measured on ccqlin038, in either direction. Every
ratio here is within-session against a floor re-derived here.

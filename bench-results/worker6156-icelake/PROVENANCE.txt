The subdirectory is named for the **TBLIS 2.0 build** it measured against, and
that is not decoration. This run used `../baselines/tblis-2.0-install`, whose
BLIS was configured with `BLIS_CONFIG_FAMILY=auto` on a Cascade Lake host and is
therefore **skx-only**: one `bli_cntx_init_skx`, 1364 `bli_` symbols, and no
skinny-GEMM kernel set. It is the build every previously committed number used,
which is why it was used here too. A one-shot look put the multi-config build of
the same source up to 1.68x ahead of it on low-arithmetic-intensity shapes, so
**the TBLIS 2.0 columns in this run are under review** and a proper A/B plus a
like-for-like counterpart run are pending. The engine's own columns, TTGT, and
TBLIS v1.3.0 are unaffected.

Quality of the measurement, which is the best in this repo so far:

  * Session-span floor (`A3` against `A`, 2.5 h apart): **0.998-1.001**, with
    **0 of 980** case-dtype-engine points outside +-6%.
  * Near floor (`A2` against `A`, minutes apart): 0.995-1.003, 1 of 980 outside.
  * Every arm reports `self 100%, co-tenants: none`. Identical arms repeated to
    within 0.4 s on 1424 s.

Note that the session-span floor is no worse than the near floor. On Zen2, drift
grew with the distance between arms (A32: 0.02% at a minute, 1-2% at an hour,
4.4% across a cold start) and the discarded warm-up arm was what made that
visible. Here the warm-up arm bought confirmation rather than a correction: an
exclusive, SMT-off Ice Lake node simply does not drift over four hours. A32's
effect looks like a property of boost headroom and co-tenancy, not a universal.

Not comparable to any number measured on `ccqlin038` or on a `rome` node.
